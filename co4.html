CO4 Unit IV · Total Station

Total Station Surveying

Course Outcome

CO4: Use total station for land surveying and plotting the details.

Introduction

A Total Station is an electronic/optical surveying instrument that integrates an Electronic Distance Measurement (EDM) device with an electronic theodolite and an onboard microprocessor. In a single sighting it measures horizontal angle, vertical angle and slope distance to a target, then computes and stores coordinates automatically — making it the workhorse instrument of modern ground-based digital surveying.

Instrument Parts

Telescope

Sights the target/prism; carries the electronic angle-measuring sensors.

EDM unit

Emits and receives an infrared or laser signal reflected off a prism to compute distance.

Keyboard & display

Used to input point codes, review readings and control on-board programs.

Tribrach & tripod

Levelling base that centres and stabilises the instrument over the station.

Prism / reflector

Mounted on a target pole at the point being observed, reflects the EDM signal back.

Onboard memory

Stores measured angles, distances and computed coordinates for later download.

Setting-Up Procedure

  1. Centring: Position the tripod so the instrument is exactly over the ground station mark, using the optical/laser plummet.
  2. Levelling: Use the tribrach's levelling screws and the circular/electronic bubble to make the vertical axis truly vertical.
  3. Powering on & orientation: Switch on the instrument, set the station coordinates and instrument height, then sight a known backsight point to orient the horizontal circle.
  4. Job/point setup: Create or select a job file, and enter point codes/descriptions for the features to be surveyed.

Measurement Principle

Once oriented, the total station computes the 3D coordinates of every observed point through simple trigonometry:

  • Distance: The EDM measures the time (or phase shift) of an infrared/laser pulse travelling to the prism and back, converting it to slope distance.
  • Angles: Electronic angle encoders in the horizontal and vertical circles measure the direction of the line of sight relative to the backsight.
  • Coordinates: The instrument resolves slope distance and vertical angle into horizontal distance and elevation difference (trigonometric levelling), then combines this with the horizontal angle to compute the point's easting, northing and elevation — all automatically, in real time.

Contouring & Mapping

By systematically observing detail points around a site — using the radial (polar) method from one or more control stations — a total station builds up a dense set of 3D coordinates. This point data is downloaded to mapping/CAD software (see CO5) where it is triangulated into a Digital Terrain Model (DTM), from which contour lines and topographic plans are generated automatically.

Sources of Error

Instrumental

Collimation error, vertical index error, and circle graduation errors from imperfect instrument adjustment.

Personal

Inaccurate centring, levelling, or bisecting of the target by the surveyor.

Natural

Atmospheric refraction, temperature and pressure variation affecting the EDM signal path.

Instrumental errors are largely eliminated by taking observations in both face-left and face-right and averaging the results, along with periodic calibration against manufacturer specifications.
